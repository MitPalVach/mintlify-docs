---
title: integer division and range narrowing mechanics
description: first, it should be determined that a direct division operation is impossible. To obtain the quotient, a multi-step mechanism should be used.
---

in the integer division scheme, we define this operation as an equation in a multidimensional space $H$, in which the main goal is to determine the cipher quotient hyperedge $\mathbf{e}_c$ from the dividend hyperedge $\mathbf{e}_a$ and the divisor hyperedge $\mathbf{e}_b$.

to simplify, we can define division as an operator equation:

$$
F(\mathbf{e}_c) = \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a) - \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) \odot \mathbf{e}_c = \mathbf{0}
$$

<Note>

	it is worth clarifying what each element of this equation means:

	- $\Gamma_\epsilon(H_a)$ and $\Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)$, are incidence matrices with noise;

	- $\odot $, a linear transformation that inverts the influence of $ \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) $ on $ \mathbf{ e }_c $,
	which is constructed using the Moore–Penrose inverse.

</Note>

to solve the division problem, it is necessary to define the mechanics of range narrowing as:

$$
\mathbf{e}_c \in \mathcal{R} = \left\{ \mathbf{e} \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid \mathbf{0} \leq \mathbf{e} \leq \mathbf{1} \right\}
$$

this is done to ensure that the quotient hyperedge $\mathbf{e}_c$ remains within the ranges of $\mathcal{R}$.

the next step is to initialize the cipher hyperedge and then define the inverse operator for the incidence matrix of the divisor.

cipher hyperedges with cubic noise elements:

$$
\mathbf{e}_a = \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a) + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_a^3, \quad \mathbf{e}_b = \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_b^3
$$

where $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_a^3$ and $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_b^3$ are the cubic noise vectors added to $H_a$ and $H_b$.

also introduce regularization parameters to reduce the noise increase due to small singular values at the points:

$$
\Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^+ = \left( \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^\top \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) + \alpha I \right)^{-1} \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^\top
$$

in this case, $\Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^\top$ is a transposition of $\Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)$, $I$ is the identity matrix of the corresponding size.

as a result, the division operation can be expressed as:

$$
\mathbf{e}_c = \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^+ \odot \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a)
$$

let's substitute the operator $\odot$:

$$
\mathbf{e}_c = \mathcal{T} \cdot \mathcal{S} \cdot \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^+ \cdot \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a)
$$

we continue to expand:

$$
\mathbf{e}_c = \mathcal{T} \cdot \mathcal{S} \cdot \left( \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^\top \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) + \alpha I \right)^{-1} \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^\top \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a)
$$

a further goal is to ensure that $\mathbf{e}_c$ is within the acceptable range $\mathcal{R}$ while minimizing the residual noise. That is, we literally apply the projection operator $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}$ to limit $\mathbf{e}_c$:

$$
\mathbf{e}_c' = \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbf{e}_c) = \min\left( \max(\mathbf{e}_c, \mathbf{0}), \mathbf{1} \right)
$$

component by component, for each $i = 1, 2, ... , n$:

$$
(\mathbf{e}_c')_i =
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } (\mathbf{e}_c)_i < 0 \\
(\mathbf{e}_c)_i & \text{if } 0 \leq (\mathbf{e}_c)_i \leq 1 \\
1 & \text{if } (\mathbf{e}_c)_i > 1
\end{cases}
$$

let's calculate the residual noise:

<Warning>

	hope it’s clear why, but if for some reason it is not clear then you can read our article on noise management [here](/tech-docs/hfhe/subtraction-in-hypergraph-space/hypergraph-stability-after-subtraction) (there we are talking about noise components after subtraction, but the logic is appropriate).

</Warning>

$$
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{res}} = \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) \odot \mathbf{e}_c' - \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a)
$$

now the criteria for assessing compliance will be determined as:

$$
\| \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{res}} \|_2 \leq \epsilon_{\text{tol}}
$$

where $\epsilon_{\text{tol}}$ is the noise tolerance threshold after which the ciphertexts will become insane.

next step iteratively refine $\mathbf{e}_c$ to minimize residual noise:

$$
\mathbf{e}_c^{(0)} = \mathbf{e}_c'
$$

for each iteration $𝑘 = 0 , 1 , 2 , … , 𝐾$ until $\| {\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{res}}^{(k)}} \|_2 \leq \epsilon_{\text{tol}}$:

$$
\mathbf{e}_c^{(k+1)} = \mathbf{e}_c^{(k)} + \Delta \mathbf{e}_c^{(k)}
$$

where $\Delta \mathbf{e}_c^{(k)}$ is defined as:

$$
\Delta \mathbf{e}_c^{(k)} = \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^+ \odot \left( \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a) - \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) \odot \mathbf{e}_c^{(k)} \right) + \beta_k \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c^{(k)^3}
$$

<Note>

	in this case, $\beta_k$ is a scaling factor to control the influence of noise on iterations $k_n$.

</Note>

additive type of error is:

$$
\mathbf{r}^{(k)} = \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a) - \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) \odot \mathbf{e}_c^{(k)}
$$

cubic noise correction:

$$
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c^{(k)^3} = \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c^{(k)} \circ \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c^{(k)} \circ \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_c^{(k)}
$$

the next step is to recalculate the residual noise after the update:

$$
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{res}}^{(k+1)} = \| \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) \odot \mathbf{e}_c^{(k+1)} - \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a) \|_2
$$

we will also check the compliance with tolerances:

$$
\| \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{res}}^{(k+1)} \|_2 \leq \epsilon_{\text{tol}}
$$

<p style={ { fontStyle: 'italic', fontWeight: 'bold' } }>
	it is reasonable to ask how the stability of the structure is determined. Recall that the incidence matrix has good
	rows provided that:
</p>

$$
\epsilon_{ij} \leq \frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{r^2} - \frac{2 \ln r}{r}
$$

and also the definition of the kernel norm:

$$
k_i(\Gamma(\Upsilon)) \leq 6 + \frac{37}{r} + O\left( \frac{1}{r^2} \right) \sum_{j=1}^{r} \epsilon_{ij}^2
$$

as was said earlier, to guarantee the stability of the hypergraph structure in the presence of cubic noise, the inequality must be satisfied for good strings with large $r$ (more details were described [here](/tech-docs/hfhe/subtraction-in-hypergraph-space/minimizing-deviations-in-hypergraph-matrices)):

$$
h_i(\Gamma(\Upsilon)) - k_i(\Gamma(\Upsilon)) \geq \frac{r^2}{4} \sum_{\substack{j=1 \\ \epsilon_{ij} < 0}}^{r} \epsilon_{ij}^2 + 2r \sum_{\substack{j=1 \\ \epsilon_{ij} > 0}}^{r} \epsilon_{ij}^2
$$

after iterative refinement, the final chiper hyperedge $\mathbf{e}_c$ is described as follows:

$$
\mathbf{e}_c = \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^+ \odot \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a) + \sum_{k=1}^{3} \beta_k \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^k
$$

$$
\mathbf{e}_c = \mathcal{T} \cdot \mathcal{S} \cdot \left( \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^\top \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) + \alpha I \right)^{-1} \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^\top \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a) + \sum_{k=1}^{3} \beta_k \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^k
$$

$$
\mathbf{e}_c = \mathcal{T} \cdot \mathcal{S} \cdot \left( \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^\top \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b) + \alpha I \right)^{-1} \Gamma_\epsilon(H_b)^\top \Gamma_\epsilon(H_a) + \beta_1 \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^1 + \beta_2 \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^2 + \beta_3 \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^3
$$

finally, let's define a limit for the total accumulated noise:

$$
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{k=1}^{3} \beta_k \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^k \leq \epsilon_{\text{max}}
$$
